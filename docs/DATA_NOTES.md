# Data notes

Things about EDGAR that were not obvious until the data was in front of us.
Hand-written and stable; [`EVALUATION.md`](EVALUATION.md) is regenerated by the
probe on every run and should not be edited.

---

## 1. The tag-mapping problem is real, and it is mostly revenue

The premise of this project is that XBRL supplies an answer key. That only holds
if a business concept can actually be resolved to a tag. Measured across 55
companies (see `EVALUATION.md` for the full table):

| Field | Distinct `us-gaap` tags needed | Resolution, FY2023 |
|---|---|---|
| `total_revenue` | **4** | 100% |
| `net_income` | 2 | 100% |
| `total_assets` | 1 | 100% |
| `operating_cash_flow` | 2 | 100% |

Revenue is the outlier, and the distribution shows why a single-tag lookup is
not good enough:

| Tag | Companies, FY2023 |
|---|---|
| `RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax` | 28 |
| `Revenues` | 22 |
| `RevenuesNetOfInterestExpense` | 3 |
| `RevenueFromContractWithCustomerIncludingAssessedTax` | 2 |

**Taking only the most common tag would have resolved 28 of 55 companies — 51%.**
The mapping in `config/xbrl_tag_map.yaml` takes it to 100%. The three banks
using `RevenuesNetOfInterestExpense` are not an edge case to paper over: gross
revenue is not a meaningful line for a bank, so the tag difference is a real
accounting distinction, not a filer inconsistency.

## 2. Fiscal year cannot be read off the `fy` field

Each observation in `companyfacts` carries `fy` and `fp`. They describe **the
filing the fact appeared in**, not the period the fact covers. A FY2022
comparative sitting inside a FY2024 10-K carries `fy: 2024`.

The resolver therefore keys on the calendar year the period *ends* in, which
also handles non-calendar fiscal years correctly: Apple's FY2023 runs
2022-09-25 to 2023-09-30 and is FY2023.

## 3. Period shape has to be checked, or the answer key is silently 4x wrong

Revenue, net income and operating cash flow are *duration* facts; total assets
is an *instant* fact. `companyfacts` mixes annual and quarterly durations in the
same array. Accepting a Q4 duration as the annual figure produces an answer key
roughly 4x too small — and nothing crashes. Every correct extraction would then
be scored wrong.

The resolver accepts a duration only if it spans 340–400 days, which admits
52-week (364) and 53-week (371) retail years while excluding quarters.

## 4. Restatements mean "ground truth" needs a point of view

The same fiscal year appears in `companyfacts` many times: once as originally
reported, then again as a comparative in every later filing, occasionally
restated. FY2023 revenue was restated by at least one later filing for **7 of 55
companies**.

Scoring an extraction read out of the original 10-K against a figure restated
two years later would penalise the model for correctly reproducing what the
document said. So the resolver prefers the value **as originally reported**,
flags `restated=True` when later filings disagree, and accepts an `accession`
to pin the answer to one specific filing.

## 5. Ticker to CIK is not stable across corporate reorganisations

EDGAR's `company_tickers.json` always points at the **current** registrant. When
a company reorganises, the ticker migrates to a newly-registered entity whose
XBRL history begins at the reorganisation, while the filings for the study
period stay under the predecessor CIK — which by then carries no ticker at all.

Two of 55 companies in this corpus are affected:

| Ticker | Ticker map resolves to | History begins | Filings for 2022–24 are under |
|---|---|---|---|
| XOM | ExxonMobil Holdings Corp (CIK 2115436) | 2025 | EXXON MOBIL CORP (CIK 34088) |
| BLK | BlackRock, Inc. (CIK 2012383) | 2024-02-20 | BlackRock Finance, Inc. (CIK 1364742) |

This surfaced as the resolution probe failing **all four fields** for XOM — a
useful reminder that a field failing across the board points at the entity, not
the tag map. `CIK_OVERRIDES` in `scripts/build_universe.py` records the
resolved answers; `_history_span` catches the general case.

BLK genuinely straddles the window: FY2022 and FY2023 belong to the predecessor,
FY2024 to the successor. Only one CIK per company is carried, so BLK's FY2024 is
reported as unresolvable rather than quietly stitched together. That is the
single unresolved cell in the FY2024 table.

## 6. `filings.recent` is not a filing history

The obvious coverage check — "does this company have a 10-K in `filings.recent`
for the target years?" — is wrong, and wrong in a way that singles out the
largest filers.

`filings.recent` is capped at roughly the most recent 1000 filings. Banks that
issue structured notes blow straight through it:

| Company | Filings in `recent` | Period actually covered | Dominant form |
|---|---|---|---|
| JPMorgan | 26,065 | 12 months | 22,635 × `424B2` |
| Bank of America | 11,358 | 12 months | 10,558 × `424B2` |

Their 10-Ks are paged out into `filings.files`. The first version of the
coverage check reported "no 10-K in 2022–2024" for JPM, BAC, GS, MS and WFC —
five false positives, all banks.

The fix needs no extra requests: `filings.files` carries `filingFrom` and
`filingTo` for every page, so the span of an entity's history can be read
straight out of the payload the check already has.
